# Fragility Score: Mapping Counterfactual Openings in Soccer Defenses

**Introduction.** Defensive organization is fundamental to limiting attacking opportunities in football. Tracking-based approaches quantify spatial control ([Martens et al., 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2021.676179/full)) and defensive pressure ([Forcher et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1080/24733938.2022.2158213)). Yet an organized defense may still leave nearby positional opportunities for attackers. This motivates a counterfactual perspective: rather than evaluating only observed positions, we examine whether small changes in an attacker's position could increase attacking control. We introduce Structural Opening Gain (SOG), a deterministic algorithm that scores and ranks these hypothetical movements without learned scoring parameters.

**Methods.** Using PFF FC 2022 World Cup events and tracking, we examined positions 0.5–1.5 m away in eight directions for each eligible off-ball attacker, holding other player states fixed (Figure 1). SOG ranks these movements by modeled control gain, emphasizing gains in less-controlled space; Figure 2 gives the scoring rule. Separate logistic classifiers test whether the resulting opening landscape and its alignment with player movement add information beyond observed context. Figure 2 defines the baseline and added features. Classifiers were developed on 16 matches and frozen before testing on 16 held-out matches. The target is controlled advantageous penetration following an action released within two seconds. Centered velocities include subsequent positions; evaluation is retrospective.

**Results.** On held-out matches, landscape and movement-alignment features improved classifier discrimination and event retrieval, whereas the scalar SOG summary alone added essentially nothing. The full classifier increased average precision from 0.048 to 0.060 and retrieved 29.4% more distinct penetrations at the same 10% review budget. Figure 2 compares all models on the same match situations used for model development. Separately, Gold20 comprises 20 sequences highlighted in external tactical analyses, including FIFA Training Centre reports. Its 13 primary sequences supply 20 evaluable player–direction actions documented by FIFA, Total Football Analysis, and Coaches' Voice. SOG recovered 13/20 in the top decile versus 11/20 for plain pitch-control gain (difference +10 percentage points; 95% case-bootstrap interval, 0–24) within a timing-tolerant window. Pre-release recovery tied at 10/20; both additional SOG recoveries were post-release-only.

**Conclusion.** SOG maps nearby positional opportunities, rather than indicating that a defense has already been beaten. Its landscape and movement alignment improve retrospective retrieval for tactical review; neither a universal scalar defensive-quality measure nor causal effects of hypothetical movements are established.
